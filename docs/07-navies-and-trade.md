# Navies and Trade

## Overview

Historically maintaining a strong navy was necessary for trade and defense while at the same time allowing countries to project power far abroad. Yet in all Paradox Games released to date this is not the case. Navies are necessary only for island and trade focused countries and for all others they are often seen as a luxury investment only made out of roleplay or when the player gets so strong nothing matters anymore. There are many factors that combine to produce this unsatisfying result:

- **Naval Wars are all or nothing:** The lack of notable differences in terrain, real logistics or varied objectives all lead to doomstacking where the larger stack wins and the weaker power does what is sensible to do in such a situation - refuse to engage again.
- **Navies only have limited ways to affect land:** Outside of blockades naval supremacy just does not *do much*. There are no navigable rivers to sail up, blocking access, disrupting trade or transporting troops inland. The role of navies in logistics is also limited, though credit where credit is due this is one area where EU5 has attempted to make improvements.
- **Navies and Trade are practically separate systems:** before railroads (and actually, even with railroads) transporting goods by ship on sea or on rivers was vastly cheaper than on land. The *Trade Range* mechanic does represent this to an extent, but mostly trade still happens unseen or ‘off map’ with the lines going on the map simply representing something happening. Navies don't transport goods, transport ships either sit idle in port, or sit idle off the coast passively providing *Maritime Presence* instead of **actively participating in trade**. The fact that most *Trade Capacity* and *Trade Advantage* is provided by buildings rather than navies also makes interacting with other countries’ trade through competition or piracy something that is done through passive modifiers instead of actual on-map fleet movements.

Fortunately while EU5 has not quite managed to solve the above problems, the systems that exist in the game are a suitable base to build upon. Instead of focusing on the issues, let’s now outline w**hat the desired end state looks like:**

- **Foreign Trade** should primarily depend on the presence of *Light Ships* for *Trade Advantage* and the presence of *Transport Ships* for *Trade Capacity*. *Foreign Buildings* should still play a role, but their effects should be strengthened and the limits on their number severely restricted. This would allow competitors to fight over control of trade by chasing the opponents Trade Fleets away. For the Naval Game as a whole this could provide objectives other than fighting the enemy fleet or blockading the enemy coast, thus pulling the fleet in multiple directions, hopefully **opening the door to tactics other than doomstacking.**
- **By tweaking the Advancements system** along with Age Focuses we should **make it viable for land powers to invest into navy** without requiring a level of investment that cripples their efforts on land (which will always rightfully be the main priority for continental powers). But more on this in the *Technology Section* later.
- Making rivers *navigable*, though desirable, is *beyond our possibilities* as modders. We can still make navies affect land directly in a significant way by making the *Siege Penalty* of besieging an **unblockaded coastal fort** 2 instead of 1, this should be done carefully though, as it should not quite make it impossible (could do siege ability instead). The speed of land units being slower could in turn make **naval transport** more appealing. **Blockades** can be made more effective through tweaks to the *War Exhaustion* system, but that is for another chapter as well.

## Trade Dynamism

**EU4**, as simple as it was, **had a better Trade system** in practice than its successor. **Countries competed directly over control of trade flows** and navies could directly participate in a meaningful way. In contrast in EU5 most of the *Trade Capacity* in *Foreign Markets* is derived from *Trade Offices*, which are mostly not touchable by competitors (often even the Host Country is unable to do anything about them, and don’t get me started on how ridiculous that is). This leads to a situation where each country does its own thing and while there is *some* interaction it is much more passive and indirect. Solving this will not be done through a singular change but by tweaking multiple related systems to generate the desired end result:

- It is currently allowed to build in foreign countries by default. While there are other issues with this, the main relevant one is that **in practice this leads to only negative player interaction**. The only time players discuss regarding this, is when someone disallows building either due to trade or concerns over building limits (not even mentioning the bugs that come with this). **By disallowing players from building** in other countries by default and providing ways to *ask for permission*, or *pay for permission* we instead create a situation where **it is up to players to negotiate** (where both sides can benefit!) instead of reacting. In case someone is allowed to build by having paid for the privilege this relation should not be breakable for a time (10-25 years?) unless war breaks out between the parties.
- Before we can get to how navies should interact with trade, we have to address **Trade Offices** themselves. They are overall too strong, while at the same time being very spammy and thus building each of them is very unsatisfying. They should have **10x** their current **effect**, **10x** their current **price**, and be limited to **only buildable in Cities** (or in the case of Market Centers also if they are in a town or rural as a special case). Importantly, **only a single Trade Office** should be **buildable per location**, so whoever gets there first has the spot! To make competition over this possible, as part of a peace deal for 6 warscore each the player could force an opponent to destroy them. In order to make the scaling appropriate, as the Ages go by this cost should be reduced by 1 each age, reaching 1 by the *Age of Revolutions*. Of course, a *Trade Conflict* casus belli should allow for this to be selected!
- As the most important change, **Transport Ships that are in a coastal sea zone** belonging to a particular Market **should contribute Trade Capacity to their own country in said Market**. This would allow trading countries to shift their trade capacity around in whatever way it is profitable but also to knock rivals out by chasing their fleets away. Not only would this make trade dynamic again, it would make it possible for players to directly interact with each other and with the system once again! I do not have a way to evaluate if making this change is technically possible or not, but if it is, in my opinion it has huge promise to make not just trade, but also naval gameplay as a whole a lot more interesting. To discuss some specifics:
  - Trade Capacity per ship could look something like the following (from Age I through VI):
    - **I.** 0.5 / **II.** 1 / **III.** 1.5 / **IV.** 3 / **V.** 5 / **VI.** 10
  - To keep a singular market from being swarmed the maximum Trade Capacity per Market possible to achieve this way should also be limited per Age:
    - **I.** 25 (50x) / **II.** 50 (50x) / **III.** 150 (100x going forward) / **IV.** 300 / **V.** 500 / **VI.** 1000
  - The idea is to let Market Buildings be more cost effective (considering maintenance and build cost) but only barely, while they also give other bonuses as well. Maths could be off especially considering the effects of trade maintenance, trade efficiency, naval maintenance etc so values here will require monitoring to see how they are in practice.
